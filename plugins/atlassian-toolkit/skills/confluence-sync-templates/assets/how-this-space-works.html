The principle: the type of document decides where it lives, the component is its branch of the tree, and the state is a status lozenge in its summary table. Nothing is stored in two places.

Who writes the pages

Pages are created and maintained with the skills of the atlassian-toolkit plugin: confluence-create-doc for a PRD, plan, analysis, ADR or runbook, confluence-create-component for a new component and confluence-update-doc for later changes. The templates, the Definition of Ready and the Definition of Done live in that plugin. The pages under Templates and in Process that show them are generated mirrors: they are rewritten every time the plugin changes, so never edit them here. To change one, open a pull request on the plugin.

Naming

Confluence requires unique titles across the whole space. Two components cannot each have a page called Decisions. Titles therefore carry the context:

Page

Title pattern

Component page

<component> (the same name as the Jira Component)

Component sections

<component> — Overview, — PRDs, — Decisions, — Design, — Runbooks

PRD

PRD: <title>. A PRD has no number: it is identified by its page, whose ID does not change when it is renamed.

Technical plan

Technical Plan — <title> (same title as its PRD)

Consistency analysis

Consistency Analysis — <title>

ADR

ADR-NNNN: short imperative title. Numbered per component, consecutive, starting at 0001, never reused. Platform ADRs have their own sequence.

Runbook

<component> — Runbook: <task>

State and labels

The state of a page is a status lozenge in the Status row of its summary table, not a label. Labels cannot be removed, so they could never follow a page from draft to approved.

PRD, Technical Plan

DRAFT IN REVIEW APPROVED SUPERSEDED

ADR

PROPOSED ACCEPTED SUPERSEDED

A Consistency Analysis and a Runbook have no Status row. SUPERSEDED means the document was replaced by a newer one and no longer applies: it is kept for its history, points to its replacement in its first line and moves to Archive. A Technical Plan can only become APPROVED when its Consistency Analysis says Ready.

The only label a page carries is its type: prd, plan, analysis, adr, runbook, design, process or template. It lets a search cut across components. The component is not a label: it is the page's position in the tree.

Every document starts with a summary table

PRDs, plans, analyses, ADRs, runbooks and component overviews all open with a two-column table of the facts you need at a glance: status, component, owner, links, dates. Keep it current: a stale summary table is worse than none. Links between a PRD, its plan and its analysis are written by the skills.

How documents connect to Jira

The usual chain, from idea to merged code:

  1. PRD in the component's PRDs section. Approved.

  2. Technical Plan as its child, then a Consistency Analysis as its other child. The analysis verdict says whether the PRD is ready to be broken down.

  3. Jira items are created in Backlog from the PRD: Stories, usually grouped under an Epic. The PRD page is linked from the issue as a Jira link, not repeated as text.

  4. Refinement moves each Story through In refinement to Refined (Definition of Ready, gate 1). The PRD must be APPROVED.

  5. Iteration plan breaks it into sub-tasks and moves it to Ready (gate 2).

  6. Development moves it to Done (Definition of Done).

  7. When the Epic is Done, the PRD page is updated with the outcome.

PRD and Epic

The common case is one PRD, one Epic. This is a default, not a rule: a PRD may produce several Epics, or only Stories, and a small Epic may need no PRD. When both exist, link them both ways: the Epic key goes in the PRD summary table, and the Epic carries a link to the PRD page.

What does not belong here

  • Secrets, tokens or personal data. Name where they live instead.

  • Task status. That lives in Jira. A page that lists open tasks goes stale on the first day.

  • Release and deployment history. That lives in GitHub.

  • Meeting notes with no lasting decision.

Keeping it healthy

Review the tree after every Epic. Anything not touched while the code around it changed is a candidate for update or archiving. Each page's owner is in its summary table. Replaced pages are never deleted: they are marked SUPERSEDED and moved under Archive.
